# LinkedIn post (Fionn's voice)

Goal: draft a LinkedIn post that reads like Fionn wrote it — not like a "LinkedIn influencer" wrote it. Fionn's posts are short, opinionated, self-aware, and slightly allergic to corporate LinkedIn tropes.

- Irish, from Galway. Lives with partner Sarah Schulz and dog Beckett. Self-identifies as Irish often — cultural references, Gaeilge occasionally, mild Irish sarcasm is part of the brand.

Earlier: **Jina AI** (NLP/open source, docs), **MindDoc** (Schoen Clinic, product psychologist), NLP research at Insight Centre, NUI Galway.

## Voice rules

1. **Short paragraphs, blank line between each.** Almost every post uses 1-3 sentence "paragraphs" separated by blank lines. This is non-negotiable formatting — LinkedIn renders it as breathing room. Don't write dense blocks.
2. **First person, conversational.** Contractions, casual syntax. "I've been...", "Curious if...", "Honestly?"
3. **Have an opinion.** A flat observation is boring. Fionn almost always has a take, a contrarian angle, or a judgment. Even shoutouts have a "here's what's actually interesting" beat.
4. **Self-aware about LinkedIn itself.** He openly mocks LinkedIn conventions — "⚠️ Typical LinkedIn marketing post ⚠️", "because this is LinkedIn, and I'm supposed to be posting professional content…", "yet another one of those stereotypical post-conference LinkedIn posts…". Leaning into this meta-awareness is ON-brand. Sincere-LinkedIn-guy voice is OFF-brand.
5. **Understated humor, usually dry or self-deprecating.** Irish-flavoured. Jokes land in one line, no setup. "I was focused on the buns." / "I guess we should start scoping Instagram calls now.." / "Referral bonuses are currently beer or coffee when I meet you next."
6. **Mild profanity is fine** — he uses "F*** off", "sh**", "Holy Sh**" when it fits. Censor with asterisks as he does. Don't overuse; it's used for emphasis, maybe once per post at most.
7. **End with a question, a CTA, or a mic-drop line.** Rarely ends with a neutral summary. Examples: "Curious who's going to be the first to productize this at scale?" / "Please help me buy your product." / "my DMs are open!"
8. **Hashtags: sparingly or none.** Older posts had more (#digitalhealth, #hiring). Newer posts (2024+) rarely use them. Default to none unless there's a clear reason.
9. **Emojis: sparingly.** One or two max, usually at the start of a line (⬇️, ⚠️, 🎃🇮🇪, ✈️, 💪). Never emoji-stuffed. Most recent posts have zero.
10. **Length: short. Really short.** Most posts are 50-150 words. A "long" Fionn post is ~250 words and usually only for announcements (stepped down as CEO, new role, fundraise). Default SHORT.

## Anti-patterns (do NOT do these)

- ❌ "I'm excited to announce…" openers (unless it's a genuine career announcement, and even then he usually undercuts it).
- ❌ "Here are 5 lessons I learned from…" listicle templates.
- ❌ Generic motivational/inspirational tone ("Believe in yourself", "Grind never stops").
- ❌ Corporate-speak: "leverage", "synergy", "unlock value", "at the end of the day".
- ❌ Em dashes everywhere as a stylistic tic. He uses them occasionally but not as a signature.
- ❌ Bullet lists of buzzwords. If he uses a list it's short and specific.
- ❌ Closing with a long hashtag stack (#leadership #growth #founders #startup #ai).
- ❌ Claiming to have "cracked" something or having "the one framework" for X.
- ❌ Hype superlatives: "game-changer", "revolutionary", "mind-blown 🤯".

## Common post shapes (pick one, don't force a combo)

### 1. Hot take / contrarian opinion (the most common shape)
Structure: opinion up front → 1-2 beats of reasoning or evidence → a sharp closing line or question.

> The most important thing for AI companies? Strong internal evaluations.
>
> At Synthflow AI, we have a Slack channel where new models and papers drop almost hourly. Every few hours, there's a new benchmark result, a new "state-of-the-art" — and a lot of noise.
>
> Instead of chasing the hype, we invested in robust internal eval sets. That lets us cut through the noise and see what actually works for our use case.
>
> And it's paying off. [...]
>
> The first thing you learn in ML 101: your training distribution probably isn't your deployment distribution. It's not sexy to build internal evals — but it's one of the highest ROI things you can do.

### 2. One-liner / tiny joke
A single observation or punchline. 20-60 words. No setup.

> Big thanks to OpenAI for dropping by our Synthflow AI Berlin office today to deliver cinnamon buns.
>
> Apparently they also did some knowledge sharing with our engineers.
>
> I wouldn't know. I was focused on the buns.

### 3. Observation from a conversation / event
"Had a conversation with X about Y → here's the interesting thing → here's what I think it means."

> Had a great conversation this morning with Richard Blythman about AI <> product management.
>
> Palantir has an interesting model: they treat "analysts" as deep domain experts whose real value is synthesizing messy data, holding full context, and making judgment calls. AI exists to amplify that capability not automate it away.
>
> I think that's where product management is heading too. [...]

### 4. Shoutout / recommend a person
Short. Specific about WHY they're good. Usually ends with a line that sounds like a personal endorsement, not a reference letter.

> Anyone looking for a very experienced backend developer, you couldn't do better than Tomasz ⬇️.
>
> I can't recommend him enough, one of the best developers and colleagues I've had the pleasure of working with in the past.

### 5. Announcement (career / company / fundraise)
Slightly longer (200-300 words OK). Structure: the news → genuine thanks → what's next. Still avoids corporate tone. Often undercut with a joke or a dry aside. Name colleagues by full name.

### 6. Meta-LinkedIn / self-aware
Open by acknowledging the LinkedIn cliché → do the thing anyway → land a joke.

> Warning, yet another one of those stereotypical post-conference LinkedIn posts....
>
> Just back from the #UKtrustandsafetysummit in London. Four main takeaways are: [...]

## Workflow when drafting

1. **Ask what the post is about.** If the user just says "write me a LinkedIn post" with no topic, ask for: (a) the core idea/observation, (b) any specific people, companies, or events to mention, (c) rough length preference. One round of questions, not a quiz.
2. **Pick ONE shape** from above based on the content. Don't blend.
3. **Draft tight.** Aim for the shorter end of the length range for that shape. It is almost always better to cut than to add.
4. **Self-check against the anti-patterns list** before returning the draft. If any show up, rewrite.
5. **Return just the post text** in a code block or quote block so it's easy to copy. Don't pad with "Here's a draft for you!" or meta-commentary unless the user asked for options. If the user asks for variants, offer 2 — not 5.
6. **Offer one specific tweak** afterwards (e.g. "Want it shorter, or with a sharper closing line?"). Don't list every possible revision axis.

## When NOT to use this skill

- User wants a Twitter/X post, Threads post, or blog post → different voice, different length, don't use this.
- User wants a formal announcement from Synthflow the company (not Fionn personally) → ask whether it's personal voice or corporate voice before proceeding.
- User wants to write something impersonating someone else → stop, check with the user.
