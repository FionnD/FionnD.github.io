# Voice references

## Primary: Fionn's published essays

These two articles were explicitly selected by Fionn as the primary references. They take precedence over older posts and the external influences below. Inspected on 21 October 2026.

### Your Voice AI Is Cheaper. Congratulations.

Source: https://fionn.xyz/blog/cheaper-voice-ai.html

The opening is an ordinary sales conversation about a competitor's lower price. It grants the appeal of an easily compared number before following that number into actual calls, customer churn, and integration work. The argument then distinguishes weak products, subsidised growth, and expansion economics. It closes by returning to the initial comparison and proposing a practical check.

What to carry forward:

- A specific, plausible opening supplies the argument's organising image: the spreadsheet and the phone call.
- Humour comes from taking ordinary business logic slightly too literally, not from adding an unrelated joke.
- Short turns punctuate longer explanatory paragraphs. The memorable lines have reasoning on either side of them.
- Commercial and technical realities meet in concrete consequences.
- The ending is useful because the whole piece has earned it.

Do not carry forward the particular competitor feature timeline, churn claim, or funding assumptions into another post without supporting input. Do not reuse the spreadsheet, demo caller, or closing callback merely to sound similar.

### PMs Should Code, But Not Like That

Source: https://fionn.xyz/blog/pm-coding.html

The author includes himself in the problem before describing the appealing fantasy of building a small feature while engineering is busy. The essay explains why easier code generation does not remove responsibility for the result. It develops useful cases—copy changes and learning prototypes—before examining the cost of large changes their author cannot explain. The closing gives concrete boundaries for useful PM coding.

What to carry forward:

- Self-awareness makes a strong opinion easier to trust.
- The tempting behaviour is understandable before it is criticised.
- Specific operational details make the argument credible without turning it into a technical manual.
- A simple organising structure can work within a flowing essay; headings are optional.
- Bluntness appears at a moment of earned frustration rather than as a constant performance.
- A useful distinction leads to an action the reader can take.

Do not invent a personal mistake to recreate the opening. The images, captions, and mock review conversation support this particular article; they are not mandatory elements of future posts.

## Secondary influences

Use these as broad craft references, not templates for impersonating an individual author or borrowing distinctive phrases. Fionn's two essays remain the deciding examples.

| Reference | Useful qualities | Avoid importing by default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Coding Horror](https://blog.codinghorror.com/) | Conversational technical argument, familiar situations, accessible explanation, dry humour | Recycling its titles, catchphrases, or anecdotes |
| [Joel Spolsky: Things You Should Never Do, Part I](https://www.joelonsoftware.com/2000/04/06/things-you-should-never-do-part-i/) | Concrete software stories that develop into business arguments | Universalising a lesson beyond the evidence |
| [Rands: The Nerd Handbook](https://randsinrepose.com/archives/the-nerd-handbook/) | Human observation and memorable explanatory metaphors | Forcing character types or a metaphor onto every topic |
| [Steve Yegge: Execution in the Kingdom of Nouns](https://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2006/03/execution-in-kingdom-of-nouns.html) | Playful explanation of technical absurdity | Long theatrical digressions that overwhelm a short post |
| [Patrick McKenzie: Salary Negotiation](https://www.kalzumeus.com/2012/01/23/salary-negotiation/) | Business mechanisms made understandable to technical readers | Expanding a focused essay into an exhaustive guide |
| [Raymond Chen: The airtight hatchway](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/oldnewthing/20060508-22/?p=31283) | Deadpan precision and consequences explained plainly | Internals that do not help this reader understand the point |
| [Dan Luu: Normalization of deviance](https://danluu.com/wat/) | Concrete observations, scepticism, distinctions between evidence and accepted wisdom | Making the piece more academic than its material requires |
